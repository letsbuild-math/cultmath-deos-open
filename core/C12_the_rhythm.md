# C12 · The Rhythm

**Module:** Core · **Version:** 1.0 · **From DEOS:** cm_16, cm_06, cm_29, cm_133

> **Close the day, review the week, check the system every month, and keep the records current.**

## When to use

- Every working day, week and month. The rhythm is what keeps every other standard running.

## The standard

### Daily close (5–10 minutes, end of the working day)

1. **What moved today.** Specific things, not activity.
2. **What gave me energy.** Name it even on a bad day.
3. **Energy at close:** high, medium or low.
4. **Tomorrow's priority.** One sentence. Not a list.
5. **Open loop.** The one unresolved thing your mind will keep returning to, or "none."
6. **Recovery taken.**

Fill in the first three before setting tomorrow's priority. No daily score: it turns a passing feeling into a permanent number.

**Watch for:** the same open loop three days running (it is now an avoided decision); three low-energy closes in a row; a hard day with no recovery.

### Weekly review (Friday, 30–45 minutes)

1. **Recap:** what was completed, the most important development, what carries forward (chosen, not drifted).
2. **Decision range check (about 5 minutes):** tag the week's decisions by type (strategy, money, revenue, product, legal, risk, people, self). Which types did you avoid? Range is good. Too many separate bets is not.
3. **Next week:** one governing priority and one decision type to practice on purpose.

### Monthly health check (20–60 minutes)

Score each 1–3, and write one honest sentence each:

1. **Rhythm:** did the daily and weekly reviews run?
2. **Traceability:** can you trace your big decisions to a record?
3. **Sequence:** did work follow the standards, or were steps skipped under pressure?
4. **Failure speed:** were failures named within a day?
5. **Control vs. chase:** how many weeks felt chosen rather than reactive?
6. **Regeneration:** is anything new growing?
7. **Documentation accuracy:** do your records match reality?
8. **Human outcome:** did your decisions leave the people they touched better off?

Read 6 and 8 on their own line. A high total cannot cover a zero on either.

### Keeping records current

After any session that changed a decision, a standard or a record, update the record that same day. A stale record is worse than none, because it is trusted.

## Done when

- Daily: tomorrow's priority is written.
- Weekly: one priority and one practice target are written.
- Monthly: eight scores, eight sentences, and the one metric to improve.

## Common failures

- **The review becomes a task list.** It is a reflection, then a choice.
- **Skipping the energy field when tired.** That is when it matters most.
- **Monthly scores without sentences.** The sentence is the useful part.
