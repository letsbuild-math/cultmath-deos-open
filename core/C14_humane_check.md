# C14 · The Humane Check

**Module:** Core · **Version:** 1.0

> **Before a decision that lands on other people leaves your hands, check that it leaves them better off, not just you.**

A decision can be well reasoned, well evidenced and strategically sharp, and still be wrong, because it wins for the person making it and costs the people it lands on. The other Core standards check that a decision is *sound*. This one checks that it is *humane*. It takes about thirty seconds, so it can run in the middle of a hard day.

## When to use

- Any decision that touches another person and is hard to undo (C2 tells you which those are).
- Any hard ask: of a client, a partner, a colleague, a team, someone you depend on.
- Any recommendation that wins for one party by quietly costing another.
- Any moment you notice you are deciding fast, tired or angry.

Easy two-way decisions don't need it. One-way doors always do.

## The standard

1. **Four principles sit under the check:**
   - **Agency.** You make the call. A market, a metric or a tool can inform it; none of them decides for you.
   - **Reason and evidence.** Decide from what the evidence shows, not from habit, dogma or whatever showed up first.
   - **Real effect on real people.** Judge a decision by what it does to the wellbeing and dignity of the people it lands on, not by how it looks, scores or reads in a deck.
   - **Self-authored purpose.** You define what "enough" and "worth it" mean. A number someone else keeps moving does not.
2. **One rule about other people matters most: offer, don't impose.** Respect each person's autonomy, timeline and way of working. Support their decision; don't steer it. Don't force your solution, pace or roadmap onto someone who didn't ask for it.
3. **Offering is not avoiding.** Telling someone a hard truth they need is respect. Holding it back to keep the peace means you have decided, for them, that they can't handle it. That fails this standard too.
4. **Run the four questions, in order, before you release the decision:**

   | # | Question | What a "no" looks like |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | **Reason.** Am I thinking, or reacting? | You are rushed, angry or exhausted. Settle first: step away, breathe, sleep on it if you can. |
   | 2 | **Empathy.** Who does this land on, and am I stretching any of them past their limit? | Someone has to absorb a cost they can't recover from: trust, money, time, standing. |
   | 3 | **Autonomy.** Am I offering, or imposing? | You are overriding their judgment or timeline with yours, *or* avoiding a hard truth and calling it kindness. |
   | 4 | **Legacy.** Will this leave the situation and the people in it better than I found them? | The honest answer is "better for me, worse for them." |

5. **Every "yes": release the decision. Any "no": go back.** A "no" is not a verdict on you. It tells you which part was not thought through: the evidence, the people, or your own state. Rework that part and run the check again.
6. **The stretch test (for question 2).** People and relationships are like springs. Within their limit, strain passes and they return to where they were. Past it, the damage stays: trust turns into resentment. Ask whether the ask stays within the limit. The lasting fix is not to pull harder. It is to build the trust, clear communication and kept commitments that let the relationship carry more.
7. **Write the check into the record.** For a decision logged with C1, add one line: who it lands on and how the check came out.

## How to run it

1. Name the decision in one line, and the people it lands on.
2. Ask question 1. If it's a "no," stop and settle before going on.
3. Ask questions 2 to 4 honestly, one sentence each.
4. All "yes": release it and record the check in the decision record (C1).
5. Any "no": change the decision, the timing or the way it's delivered, then run the check again.

## Done when

- The people the decision lands on are named.
- Each of the four questions has a one-line answer.
- Any "no" changed something before the decision went out.
- The result is in the decision record.

## Common failures

- **Deciding from a depleted state.** The check run tired, angry or rushed. The decision comes out clever, not wise.
- **Winning the decision, losing the relationship.** Stretching someone past their limit because the numbers worked.
- **Interference dressed as help.** Pushing your answer and your pace onto someone because you're sure you're right.
- **Kindness only when it's easy.** Warmth that disappears exactly when pressure makes it matter. Kindness is a choice, and it fits alongside high standards and a hard line.
- **Using this standard to avoid things.** Withholding a hard truth and calling it empathy, refusing a needed boundary and calling it kindness, or not deciding and calling it respect for autonomy. Honesty is part of the check, not an exception to it.

## Template

```markdown
**Decision:** [what] · **Lands on:** [who]

1. Reason: am I thinking, or reacting? [yes/no — one line]
2. Empathy: anyone stretched past their limit? [no one / who, and how]
3. Autonomy: offering or imposing? Any hard truth held back? [one line]
4. Legacy: better than I found it, for them too? [one line]

**Result:** [released / reworked: what changed]
```
