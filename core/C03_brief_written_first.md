# C3 · Brief Written First

**Module:** Core · **Version:** 1.0

> **Write down what you expect to find, and what would prove you wrong, before you look.**

## When to use

- Any research, analysis or test whose result will inform a decision.
- Before you open a dataset, run a survey, interview a customer or ask an AI to research something.

## The standard

The brief has seven fields. All seven are filled in before the first piece of evidence is collected.

1. **The question**, written as a question. "What share of buyers think of us when ordering for a group?" Not "Group ordering."
2. **The decision it serves, and who makes it.** If no decision changes, do not run the research.
3. **Two to four candidate explanations.** One must be the boring one (nothing is happening). One must be the one you would not want to be true.
4. **Your priors,** in plain probability words: almost no chance, very unlikely, unlikely, roughly even, likely, very likely, almost certain.
5. **The falsifier.** The specific result that would make you drop your leading explanation.
6. **The stopping rule.** When you stop collecting: a count, a date, or a budget.
7. **The repeating unit.** The thing you will count: a buyer, a store, a filing, a question. If you cannot name it, you cannot count it.

**The expectation test.** Before you run it, write the result you expect. If the actual result could not have differed from that, the research was not needed.

**Source rules.**

- Record where each source came from, from its address, not from memory.
- Write one sentence on what each source can and cannot tell you.
- Name the gap: what no source you have can answer.
- Flag any source with a stake in the answer.
- Prove a claim from the primary source, not a summary of it.
- A finding from one instrument is not confirmed until a second, different method agrees.

## How to run it

1. Fill in the seven fields.
2. Write the expected result.
3. Save the brief with today's date. Do not edit it after you start.
4. Run the research.
5. Report the result against the brief: which explanation survived, whether the falsifier fired, and what you got wrong.

## Done when

- The brief is dated before the first piece of evidence.
- The findings name which candidate explanation survived and why.

## Common failures

- **The brief is written after the findings.** It will always be right. That is the problem.
- **Only one explanation.** Then every result confirms it.
- **No stopping rule.** You keep looking until you find what you wanted.
- **Changing the question mid-study** without saying so. Write a new brief.
