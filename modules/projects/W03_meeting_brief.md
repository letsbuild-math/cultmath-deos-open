# W3 · Meeting Brief

**Module:** Projects & People · **Version:** 1.0

> **Walk into every important meeting with a private live brief, and, when it helps, one shared visual.**

## When to use

- Any meeting where something is being decided, sold, negotiated or reviewed.

## The standard

### The live brief (private, for you only)

Six elements, one page:

1. **The opening question:** the first thing you will ask, chosen to get them talking about what matters to them.
2. **A checklist:** what you must learn or agree before the meeting ends. Tick as you go.
3. **Signals to watch:** what would tell you they are ready, hesitant or opposed.
4. **Ready lines:** two or three sentences prepared for the moments you expect: the objection, the price question, the "send me something."
5. **Posture reminder:** one line on how to carry yourself (for example, "listen more than talk," "do not discount").
6. **Close triggers:** what has to be true to ask for the next step, and what that step is.

### The shared visual (optional, shown to them)

- **Ask first:** would a shared visual increase understanding in this conversation? If not, skip it.
- **One object:** a single diagram, table or page they can point at.
- **Built for their decision,** not your credentials.
- **Simple enough to understand in ten seconds.**
- **Leave space for them to change it.** The best ones get marked up in the meeting.
- **Types:** a chain (M3), a comparison, a timeline, a map of options.

## How to run it

1. Write the live brief the day before.
2. Decide whether a shared visual helps. If yes, build one.
3. Bring both. Use the checklist during the meeting.
4. Afterward, capture what happened (W5 or R8).

## Done when

- Every checklist item is ticked or carried to a dated follow-up.
- The next step is agreed.

## Common failures

- **Preparing what to say,** not what to learn.
- **A visual that is really a slide deck.**
- **No close trigger,** so the meeting ends with "let's stay in touch."
