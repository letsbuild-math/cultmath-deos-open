# W9 · Writing About Your Own Work

**Module:** Projects & People · **Version:** 1.0

> **Write about your own work with the confidence its record earns, and not a word more.**

## When to use

- Self-reviews, resumes, LinkedIn posts, bios, case studies, promotion cases, interview answers, pitches about your team.

## The standard

1. **Name the flinch.** Most people undersell their own work because claiming it feels like bragging. Underselling is not modesty; it is an inaccurate report.
2. **Honest signaling.** State what happened, your role in it, and the result, plainly.
3. **The agent stance.** Write about yourself the way a good agent would describe a client: specific, confident, factual.
4. **Sell the outcome, not the process.** "Cut onboarding from three weeks to four days," not "led a cross-functional onboarding initiative."
5. **Keep an evidence file.** Every claim you make about yourself is backed by something you can produce: a number (M5), a document, a quote from someone with no stake in it.
6. **Guardrails:**
   - No promises about someone else's future. Confidence about your record only.
   - If it is not in the evidence file, it does not ship.
   - The test: would this still work if the reader knew exactly what you were doing? If the persuasion depends on them not seeing how it works, rewrite it.
7. **Respect confidentiality.** Name employers' and clients' work only as far as you are allowed to.

## How to run it

1. List what you did, with results.
2. Back each with evidence. Drop what you cannot back.
3. Rewrite each as outcome, role, number.
4. Run the "would it work if they knew" test.

## Done when

- Every claim traces to the evidence file.
- Every claim states your role.

## Common failures

- **"Helped with" and "supported"** when you led.
- **Claiming a team result as yours alone.**
- **Confidential details** shared to sound impressive.

## Template

```markdown
| Claim (outcome) | Your role | Number / lever (M5) | Evidence | OK to share? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
```
