# R1 · Map the Ground First

**Module:** Research · **Version:** 1.0 · **From DEOS:** cm_104

> **The first thing you produce on a new job, client or venture contains no recommendation.**

## When to use

- The first two to four weeks of a new role, client, partnership or market.
- Any situation with more than two parties whose interests you do not yet know.

## The standard

1. **Your first artifact is a map, not a plan.** Recommending before you know who decides is how new people lose their first year.
2. **Five reads make the map:**
   1. **Ownership:** who owns each budget, product, channel and decision. Names, not departments.
   2. **Influence:** who can stop a decision they do not own. Often not the same people as ownership.
   3. **Relationship quality, graded honestly:** for each party, strong, workable, cold or hostile. Grade from evidence (replies, follow-through), not from how the last meeting felt.
   4. **What each party actually wants,** beyond the stated objective. A target, a promotion, safety, credit, a quiet quarter.
   5. **What you need:** the access, data, sponsors and wins you require to do the job. Written down, not assumed.
3. **Every entry on the map is sourced:** who told you, or what you saw.
4. **The map is private.** It names people's interests plainly. Do not circulate it.
5. **Recommendations start only when the map is good enough** that you can say who will say yes, who will say no, and why.

## How to run it

1. List every party you have met or heard of.
2. Fill the five reads for each. Mark gaps as gaps.
3. Close the biggest gaps in one-to-one conversations. Ask what success looks like for them this year.
4. Update the map weekly for the first month.
5. Write your first recommendation only after you can predict the reaction to it.

## Done when

- Every decision you will need has a named owner and a named blocker.
- You can predict who will resist your first recommendation, and why.

## Common failures

- **Mistaking the org chart for the map.**
- **Grading relationships by politeness.**
- **Skipping "what I need."** Then you discover it when you are already late.
