# R6 · Test a Signal

**Module:** Research · **Version:** 1.0

> **Confirm the cheap-to-fake against the expensive-to-fake before a signal counts.**

## When to use

- A trend, a viral moment, an analyst claim, a hit show, a burst of social chatter, or "everyone is talking about X" is about to change a plan.

## The standard

1. **Talk is a lead, not a fact.** Anything people said, wrote or posted is cheap to produce. Before it counts, find a second signal that would cost real money or effort to fake.

   | Market | Cheap to fake | Costly to fake |
   |---|---|---|
   | Consumer | Reviews, posts, stated interest, "what people are saying" | Real spending and behavior at scale: what people buy, adopt, watch |
   | B2B | Analyst framing, LinkedIn discussion, case studies, stated interest | A costly commitment: money spent, a contract signed, a team hired, plus a direct conversation with the buyer |

2. **Never relax this to "two independent sources."** Agreement is what gets manufactured. In a small market, three sources agreeing can be one story in three coats.
3. **If you cannot confirm it, lower your confidence and stage the commitment.** Spend a little, set a check date, and spend more only if the costly signal shows up.
4. **For a cultural read** (a hit show, a mood), run six steps:
   1. Pick a story that is big by a named chart, not because you watched it.
   2. Name the mood it carries in one plain sentence.
   3. Turn the mood into a prediction about behavior that could be wrong.
   4. Find a hard, independent fact that agrees or disagrees.
   5. Name the exact decision it would change.
   6. Log it with a check date.
5. **Watch direction and lag.** Some "leading" signals run backward or late. Ask which way cause flows.
6. **The same mood can support opposite readings.** Name the competing reads and let the costly signal decide.

## How to run it

1. Write the signal and its source.
2. Classify it: cheap or costly to fake.
3. If cheap, name the costly signal that would confirm it and go find it.
4. Decide: confirmed, unconfirmed (stage the commitment), or contradicted.
5. Log it with a check date.

## Done when

- The signal is confirmed by something costly to fake, or the commitment is staged with a check date.

## Common failures

- **Volume mistaken for truth.** A thousand posts is still talk.
- **Watching the show and calling it research.**
- **No check date,** so you never learn whether the read was right.

## Template

```markdown
| Signal | Source | Cheap / costly | Confirming signal sought | Found? | Decision changed | Commitment (full / staged) | Check date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
```
