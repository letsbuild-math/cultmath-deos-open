# R2 · Category Read

**Module:** Research · **Version:** 1.0

> **Before you advise on a category, find out how it actually decides.**

## When to use

- Entering a new category, as an employee, consultant or founder.
- Before setting targets, building a model or writing positioning for a category you have not worked in.

## The standard

1. **Answer eight questions before any recommendation:**
   1. **Who is the real customer?** The one who pays, the one who chooses, the one who uses. Often three different people.
   2. **Where does the money actually sit?** Which budget line, owned by whom.
   3. **What is the shared measurement?** The data everyone in the category trusts (a syndicated panel, a trade index, a rating).
   4. **What measures does finance recognize?** The numbers that get a budget approved.
   5. **What is the calendar,** and which dates are fixed? Trade shows, line reviews, budget cycles, menu changes, seasons.
   6. **Who else has to execute?** Distributors, agencies, retailers, sales teams.
   7. **What must be approved, and by whom, before a claim can be made?** Legal, regulatory, retailer, medical.
   8. **What is the dominant business problem right now?**
2. **Mark where every answer came from:**
   - **[V] Verified:** a named external source. May go into published work.
   - **[E] Experience:** your own operating knowledge. May shape reasoning; not stated as fact in published work.
   - **[U] Unverified:** believed true, perishable, not checked. May not go into any target, rule or published claim until verified.
3. **Answers go stale.** Date each one and note how fast it changes.
4. **Do not configure plans, targets or messaging for a category until questions 1–7 are answered.**

## How to run it

1. Draft answers to all eight from what you know. Mark each V, E or U.
2. Verify the U answers first, starting with Q1 and Q2.
3. Interview one person in each role from Q6.
4. File the read with the date. Review it each quarter.

## Done when

- Questions 1–7 answered, each marked and dated.
- No U answer feeds a target or a published claim.

## Common failures

- **Treating the end user as the customer** when someone else pays.
- **Using the measurement you like** instead of the one the category trusts.
- **Missing a fixed date** and arriving a cycle late.
