# R7 · What Buyers Actually Said

**Module:** Research · **Version:** 1.0 · **From DEOS:** cm_140

> **A phrasing you supplied is not a phrasing you found.**

## When to use

- Writing messaging, positioning, search terms, sales scripts or personas.
- Any time you need to know how buyers describe a problem, a product or a category.

## The standard

1. **Collect from four records, weighted by what they cost the buyer to produce:**

   | Record | What it is | Weight |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Say** | What they tell you when asked: surveys, panels, interviews. | Lowest. You supplied the words. |
   | **Ask** | What they typed when nobody was selling: searches, forum posts, review text, support tickets, chat questions. | High |
   | **Do** | What they wrote once it became work: job posts, RFPs, scopes of work, internal docs. | High |
   | **Pay** | What the line item is called in a budget, contract or invoice. | Highest, smallest sample |

2. **The corroboration rule.** A phrasing in both Ask and Do is real. A phrasing found only in Say is one you taught them. Log it as such and do not carry it forward.
3. **Do not discard a pattern for lacking Pay evidence. Do not promote one for a single Pay instance.**
4. **Interested parties weaken a record.** A vendor's case study quoting a customer is a Say record dressed as Pay.
5. **Record three fields per quote:**
   - **Trigger:** what made it urgent now. This predicts purchase; it is the field most people skip.
   - **Job:** what they are trying to get done.
   - **Words:** their exact phrasing, verbatim.
6. **The distribution is the finding.** Report the spread of phrasings across independent speakers, with counts. One vivid quote is an anecdote.
7. **Collect only what was public or shared with you for this purpose.** No scraping private groups, no personal data kept.

## How to run it

1. Write the brief (C3), including the stopping rule (for example, 60 independent speakers).
2. Collect from Ask and Do first. Add Say last.
3. Code each utterance: record, trigger, job, words.
4. Group the words. Count speakers per phrasing.
5. Report the top phrasings with counts and the records they appear in.

## Done when

- Each top phrasing shows its count and which records it appeared in.
- Say-only phrasings are flagged.

## Common failures

- **Running a survey and calling the answers "the customer's language."**
- **Quoting the best line** instead of the distribution.
- **No trigger field,** so you know what they want but not when they buy.

## Template

```markdown
| # | Record (Say/Ask/Do/Pay) | Source | Date | Trigger | Job | Words (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Phrasings, by speaker count:**
| Phrasing | Speakers | Records seen in | Say-only? |
|---|---|---|---|
```
