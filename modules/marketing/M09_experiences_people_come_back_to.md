# M9 · Experiences People Come Back To

**Module:** Marketing & Brand · **Version:** 1.0 · **From DEOS:** cm_91

> **You cannot create an experience. You can only design what happens at each touchpoint.**

## When to use

- Designing or fixing a customer experience: an event, a store, onboarding, a service, a launch.

## The standard

Five laws:

1. **Design stimuli at touchpoints.** The first deliverable is a touchpoint map, never a strategy deck.
2. **Consistency across touchpoints is itself the message.** An inconsistent surface says something. Run a consistency pass before launch.
3. **Experience turns into loyalty only through a sequence, and authenticity gates it.** Design the authenticity test before the experience. Measure identification ("this is for people like me"), not just satisfaction.
4. **What people remember is the peak and the end.** Design one deliberate high point and one deliberate ending.
5. **Perceived experience is cheaper to fix than actual experience.** Where friction cannot be removed, change how it feels: explain the wait, show progress.

Five stages:

| # | Stage | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Touchpoint map | Every contact point, its stimulus, owner, intended response and failure mode |
| 2 | Authenticity gate | The test this audience applies, and where you would fail it |
| 3 | Consistency pass | Contradictions found across every surface |
| 4 | Peak and end design | One designed high point, one designed ending |
| 5 | Measurement | What you will observe, decided before you can see the answer |

## How to run it

Run the five stages in order. Do not design the peak before the map exists.

## Done when

- Every touchpoint has an owner and an intended response.
- One peak and one ending are designed.
- Measures are written before launch.

## Common failures

- **Uniformly pleasant, nothing memorable.**
- **A weak ending,** such as a confusing bill or checkout.
- **Measuring only satisfaction.**
