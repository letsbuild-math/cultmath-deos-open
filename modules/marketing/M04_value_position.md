# M4 · Value Position

**Module:** Marketing & Brand · **Version:** 1.0

> **Find where the buyer's willingness to pay is set today, what set it there, and what would move it.**

## When to use

- Before a price change, a repositioning, a premium line, or a "why won't they pay more" conversation.

## The standard

1. **The question:** where is willingness to pay currently set, what set it there, and what decisions would move it?
2. **It ends in one of three verdicts:**
   - The position is fine. Price and value match; look elsewhere.
   - **The position is the constraint.** Buyers see you as a cheaper or narrower thing than you are.
   - The constraint is elsewhere: distribution, product, capacity, sales.
3. **Read the evidence buyers use to set price:** what they compare you to, the words they use for you (R7), your price against the reference set, what you are bought for, and who else is in the consideration set.
4. **A position has four parts:** who it is for (M1), what you are, what you are chosen over and why, and the proof.
5. **Keep the read bounded.** A short, capped read (for example, 90 minutes, 2–3 pages) that ends in a verdict. A full plan is a separate piece of work.
6. **State the limits:** what public evidence cannot show.

## How to run it

1. Write the question and the decision it serves.
2. Collect the reference set: what buyers compare you with, and at what price.
3. Collect buyer language about you and the alternatives.
4. Write the verdict and the evidence for it.
5. If the position is the constraint, name the one decision that would move it.

## Done when

- One of the three verdicts, with evidence.
- If "position is the constraint," one named move.

## Common failures

- **Treating every pricing problem as a positioning problem.**
- **Comparing yourself to the competitor you want,** not the one buyers use.
