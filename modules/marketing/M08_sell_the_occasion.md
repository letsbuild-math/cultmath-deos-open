# M8 · Sell the Occasion

**Module:** Marketing & Brand · **Version:** 1.0

> **Small brands fight for a share of a shelf. Occasions create the demand that fills it.**

## When to use

- A small brand with limited shelf or menu presence.
- Several small makers or vendors who could each supply one part of the same moment: a game night, a tailgate, a team lunch.

## The standard

1. **Sell the moment, not the item.** Build a recurring occasion where your product is one natural component.
2. **Five conditions for it to work:**
   1. **A convener with standing,** whose invitation people accept without negotiating. Usually the venue or the network's center.
   2. **One component each.** Every participant supplies exactly one thing. Overlap starts a price fight.
   3. **Everyone keeps their own margin.** No pooled revenue, no shared pricing.
   4. **Exit rights stated up front.** Anyone can leave.
   5. **A recurring slot, not an event.** One night that repeats beats a festival that does not. Repetition turns an occasion into a channel.
3. **Link it to entry points** (R3). Pick an occasion that is a real buyer situation, not one you invented.
4. **Write what would disprove it** before running:
   - Participant revenue does not move beyond normal traffic (compare the same venue on an ordinary week).
   - Participants drop out by the second or third run.
   - No convener can be found other than you.

## How to run it

1. Name the occasion and the CEP it serves.
2. Find the convener.
3. Assign one component per participant.
4. Agree the rules: own margin, exit rights, the recurring slot.
5. Run it three times. Compare to ordinary weeks.

## Done when

- Three runs completed and compared against a baseline week.
- A decision recorded: continue, change or stop.

## Common failures

- **A one-off event** called a strategy.
- **Pooled pricing** that starts arguments.
- **You as the permanent convener.** Then it is your event, not a channel.
