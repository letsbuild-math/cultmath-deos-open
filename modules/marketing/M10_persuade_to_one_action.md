# M10 · Persuade to One Action

**Module:** Marketing & Brand · **Version:** 1.0 · **From DEOS:** cm_47

> **One piece, one proposition, one next step, and trust earned before it is asked for.**

## When to use

- Landing pages, ads, sales emails, pitch decks, calls to action.
- After M11 says the person is ready to move.

## The standard

1. **One piece, one proposition.** If you have two, write two pieces.
2. **Four stages, trust first:**

   | Stage | Job | Avoid | Use |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **Attention** | Earn the stop | Gimmicks, hooks for their own sake | A specific truth from their world they recognize |
   | **Interest** | Hold it | Piling on features | Name the problem better than they can |
   | **Desire** | Make them want it | Scarcity, hype, fear | Show the real gap between where they are and where they could be |
   | **Action** | Get the move | Pressure, "act now" | One clear, low-friction step they would take even with their guard up |

3. **Honest levers only:** specific proof, real social evidence, a clear risk reversal, a real deadline if one exists. Never invent urgency.
4. **Conviction, bounded by truth.** Say it plainly and with confidence; claim nothing you cannot show.
5. **On your own sales surfaces, say plainly that you are selling.**
6. **Borrowing a page that works:** take its structure, test whether that structure fits your proposition, and never copy its claims.

## How to run it

1. Write the one proposition and the one action.
2. Draft each stage in order.
3. Check every claim has proof.
4. Remove every second call to action.

## Done when

- One proposition, one primary action.
- Every claim in Desire has proof.

## Common failures

- **Three calls to action.**
- **Desire built on fear** the reader will later resent.
- **A hook unrelated to the offer.**
