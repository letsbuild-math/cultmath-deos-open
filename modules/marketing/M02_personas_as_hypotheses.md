# M2 · Personas as Hypotheses

**Module:** Marketing & Brand · **Version:** 1.0 · **From DEOS:** cm_17

> **A persona is a testable guess about one chosen segment, specific enough to settle a copy or product decision.**

## When to use

- After you have chosen a target segment (M1). Not before.
- When a team argues about copy, features or channels and nobody can say for whom.

## The standard

1. **Segment first.** A persona describes a segment you chose. Without that choice it describes everyone.
2. **A persona answers four questions:**
   1. What situation are they already in before they hear of you?
   2. What tension are they living with that you resolve?
   3. What moment makes them reach for a solution now?
   4. What must they never feel using your product, because it breaks trust for good?
3. **Three research inputs, minimum:** buyer language (R7), behavior data (what they do, not say), and at least five conversations.
4. **The persona structure:**
   1. Header: name, role, segment, date, evidence level.
   2. Who they are.
   3. The core tension.
   4. The trigger moment.
   5. Jobs to be done.
   6. What they use today.
   7. Why they will pay.
   8. The first entry point, and the path to more.
5. **Every line is marked** evidenced (with source) or assumed.
6. **It is a hypothesis.** Write what would prove it wrong. Revisit it when sales or research contradict it.

## How to run it

1. Confirm the segment.
2. Gather the three inputs.
3. Fill the eight sections. Mark each line.
4. Write the falsifier.
5. Test one decision against it: would this copy, feature or channel serve this person?

## Done when

- Each section is filled and marked.
- The persona has settled at least one real decision.

## Common failures

- **Stock-photo personas** with a name, an age and a hobby, and no tension.
- **All lines assumed.**
- **Never updated** after the market answers.
